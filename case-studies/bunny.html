Bunny: a little planner for two

Daily tasks, habits, meals and groceries usually live in four different apps, and shared plans get buried in chat threads. Bunny is a small installable planner that keeps each person's day private and gives two people one shared space, with a code-drawn bunny called Bun to greet you and celebrate your wins.

01The problem

Planning a shared life is small, personal and a bit emotional, and most planner apps are built for teams.

  • Tasks, habits, meals and groceries live in four different apps.
  • Shared lists and trip plans end up buried in chat threads.
  • Reminders feel like pressure instead of support.
  • Generic tools have no personality, so people stop opening them.
Goal

Plan days, weeks and the far future in as few taps as possible, with repeating routines that never need re-creating, reminders that reach the lock screen, and a warm, personal feel.

03The product

Four bottom tabs and a centre add button. Every screen is reachable within two taps of Today.

TODAY

The day at a glance

Tasks, routines, water, meals, groceries, bills and a weekly report card, with a progress ring and streaks.

PLANNING

Few taps, no re-entry

Quick add and one schedule picker for tasks, routines, meals and recipes: once, chosen dates, weekdays or every day.

FOOD

From plan to shop

Meal slots with repeats, recipes that rescale, a cooking mode and ingredients that flow to the grocery list.

REMINDERS

Gentle by design

Lock-screen reminders, one nudge if something is missed, a morning hello and quiet hours.

TOGETHER

Private by default

Shared list, tasks, little notes and trip or date plans. Everything else stays private.

BUN

Personality in code

A bunny mascot drawn and animated in code: greetings, moods, celebrations and small surprises.

04Key decisions

Privacy is enforced in the database

Surprise plans and the names one person picks for the other are protected with row-level security, not just hidden in the interface.

Local-first, works offline

The app shell and recent data are cached, and offline changes are queued and synced in order, so the shop with no signal is not a problem.

Reminders that do not nag

One reminder at the set time and at most one nudge 15 minutes later, with quiet hours respected.

Meals are tasks too

One done-or-missed model for meals and tasks keeps reports consistent.

Repeats are stored as rules

Only the days a person touches are saved, so editing a repeating item never leaves orphan rows.

05Requirements and documentation

I wrote the full product spec before any code. It covers goals and non-goals, a 22-screen navigation map listing every tappable element, feature behaviour, empty states and error handling, offline and sync rules, a data model, privacy rules and non-functional requirements. I then reviewed it for gaps and closed the open questions in a decisions log. The documents live in the project's private repository.

06How I built it

The process was spec first: idea, scope, written and reviewed documentation, then designs created with Claude connected to Figma over MCP and refined by hand. I divided the build into milestones and directed AI coding tools from the specs, reviewing each milestone on a real phone before moving on, with a rule of no redesigning while building. It is deployed on Vercel, with Supabase for the backend.

Front end
React 18 and TypeScript on Vite, installable PWA with a service worker and offline cache
Backend
Supabase: Postgres with row-level security, Auth, Realtime, Storage, Edge Functions and scheduled jobs
Notifications
Standard Web Push with VAPID keys, sent by one Edge Function that runs every minute
Hosting
Vercel, deployed on every push
Testing
End-to-end testing by me: unit tests, Playwright scripts per milestone and manual testing on a real phone
