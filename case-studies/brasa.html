Brasa: pickup orders, an iPad till and a kitchen that never overbooks

Pickup ordering, an iPad till and live kitchen boards for a fictional four-branch grill-bowl and wrap restaurant group. Four apps share one backend, and every channel stays in sync in real time.

Brasa Grill, its branches, people and data are fictional, and all test data is synthetic. The case study is modeled on real-world delivery experience and contains no client material. Results to date are part of the fiction.

Problem

Rush-hour phone orders, paper tickets and a kitchen with no view of its own capacity: 71% of pickups ready on time and 18 remakes per 1,000 orders.

What I did

24 interviews and rush-hour observation, a BRD with six objectives, a 72-FR SRS, 45 rules in 8 decision tables, 42 stories, 90 tests and 12 UAT scripts.

Result

In the fictional rollout, remakes fell from 18 to 4.1 per 1,000 orders and the median walk-in sale from 74 to 48 seconds.

05Key decisions

These are the calls I would expect to defend in a design review.

Kitchen minutes, not 15-minute slots

A pickup minute is offered only if the order's kitchen minutes are free: ceil(units × minutes per unit × rush factor). Each kitchen minute is a database record with a unique index, so two orders can never hold it (ADR-001).

Walk-ins are never refused

The till can always overbook. Since release 1.2, online orders may hold only 9 of the 15 kitchen minutes in each rush quarter-hour until 20 minutes before it starts (CR-006, BR-018).

No credit for removed ingredients

Removing an ingredient never changes the price. Credits saved no cost and made prices drift from the POS articles.

Receipts stay with a certified POS provider

Brasa never signs fiscal receipts. It raises one invoice per paid order in each branch's POS account through an outbox, and rounds VAT once per rate group, exactly as the POS does (ADR-003).

07Worked example

One calculation runs through the whole document set with identical numbers: the SRS, the business rules, a story's acceptance criteria, the test cases and the test data.

Order MH-0142: add-ons, removed ingredients, VAT per rate group and a tipSame numbers in every artifact
A Chicken Grill Bowl at 9.40 with Halloumi 1.50 and Garlic sauce 0.50 (red onion and coriander removed, at no credit), two Chicken Wraps at 7.90 with Extra chicken 2.20, and a Sparkling lemonade at 2.90 total EUR 34.50. VAT is extracted once per rate: 10% on 31.60 = 2.87 and 20% on 2.90 = 0.48, so 3.35 in total and 31.15 net. Per-line rounding would have given 3.36. A 5% tip of 1.725 rounds half up to 1.73, so the card is charged EUR 36.23.

10Reflection

59files, with 595 internal links and 39 diagrams validated
0requirements without a test case
7change requests, two rejected with evidence

What worked. Modelling capacity as kitchen minutes rather than time slots. It made the rush-hour rules testable, gave the kitchen a number it recognised, and turned a vague "we get overwhelmed at lunch" into a decision table.

What I'd do next. Instrument the throttle introduced in release 1.2 for a month before tuning it, and test the till reconnect path on each branch's real network rather than a lab router.
